# Suggesting a project

Use this when the user asks what to build next, or when `notes/gaps.md` shows the same
requirement coming up with nothing behind it.

## What makes a good suggestion

1. **It comes from a job description, not a list of popular projects.** Read the posting for
   what the team is short of, not what the title says. A requirement named twice, or a problem
   the company describes on its own blog, is a stronger signal than a buzzword in a skills list.
2. **It does not work against the company.** Check the idea against what the company believes
   and sells. A project that automates away its product, or sits in a crowded or legally
   sensitive category (automated screening of job applicants is one), can hurt more than it
   helps. Keep the domain and change the angle.
3. **It closes a named gap.** Point to the row in `notes/gaps.md` it closes, or the skill on the
   page it finally backs.
4. **It fits the time the user has.** A weekend or a week. Say which, and what the smallest
   version worth showing is.
5. **It leaves evidence.** A README that says what it does and does not do, tests, a number with
   a source, and ideally a live demo or a recording. That evidence becomes the facts entry.

## Shape of the answer

Offer two or three ideas. For each: one line on what it is, the gap it closes, why it suits this
company, the smallest showable version, and the rough time. Recommend one.

When the user builds it, add a facts entry (`facts.md`) before it goes on any page.
